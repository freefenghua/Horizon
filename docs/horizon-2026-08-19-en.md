### [The Amazon Tax: When Ads Cannibalize Organic Sales](https://seths.blog/2026/08/the-amazon-tax/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Seth Godin&\#x27;s article &\#x27;The Amazon tax&\#x27; describes how Amazon ads can charge publishers for clicks that would have occurred organically, using the example of his own book where the highest-yielding ad was for the search &\#x27;Seth Godin The Knot&\#x27;—a brand-term search that would likely convert without ads. The piece argues that Amazon&\#x27;s ad system capitalizes on existing demand, effectively taxing publishers for sales they would have made anyway. Community comments debate whether this is Amazon&\#x27;s fault or a marketer&\#x27;s waste of money, with some suggesting legal action for trademark infringement or fraud. The article lacks robust metrics but highlights a common e-commerce growth pitfall: bidding on brand terms can cannibalize organic conversions and inflate ad spend without incremental value.

hackernews · herbertl · Aug 18, 13:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49345263)

**「AI Technique」** The article does not detail a specific AI technique, but the underlying mechanism involves Amazon&\#x27;s ad auction and targeting algorithms that match ads to search queries, including brand terms. These algorithms optimize for ad relevance and bid, often placing ads for competitors or the same product on searches that would have resulted in organic purchases.

**「Growth Impact」** The measurable growth outcome is a potential waste of ad spend: bidding on brand terms can lead to paying for clicks that would have converted organically, increasing CAC without incremental revenue. The scale is individual book sales on Amazon, but the principle applies broadly to e-commerce. The mechanism is that Amazon&\#x27;s ad system captures demand that already exists, so advertisers pay for what they would have gotten for free.

**「Takeaway」** Growth practitioners should audit their ad campaigns for brand-term bidding and consider pausing or lowering bids on terms that match their own product names to avoid cannibalizing organic sales.
