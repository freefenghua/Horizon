This product-management commentary uses Tesla&\#x27;s October 15 next-generation Roadster demo—where the car briefly lifted off using thrusters and even drove upside down—as a hook to ask whether an AI product manager should greenlight technically impressive but commercially unproven projects. The author argues that as LLMs, agents, and multimodal models make prototyping easier, the harder question shifts from &\#x27;can we build it&\#x27; to &\#x27;is it worth building,&\#x27; and proposes three lenses: redefining the problem via first principles, understanding what a technology actually unlocks, and making commercial judgments under uncertainty. The piece cites Klarna&\#x27;s 2024 AI customer-service assistant, which reportedly handled about two-thirds of customer conversations in its first month—equivalent to roughly 700 full-time agents—and was projected to improve profit by about $40 million in 2024, while explicitly noting that figure was a projection, not independently verified actuals. It also illustrates compounding reliability risk with a simple calculation: ten sequential steps at 95% success each yield only about 59.9% end-to-end success. The article offers no growth metrics, named growth tools, or tested tactics of its own, so its value to growth practitioners is a decision framework rather than a replicable playbook.

**「AI Technique」** The piece is conceptual rather than technical, but it references LLMs, AI agents, multimodal models, and Computer Use—AI that operates a mouse and browser to work with legacy systems lacking APIs. Its most concrete technical point is reliability math: chaining ten steps at 95% individual success gives roughly 59.9% end-to-end success, and real-world failures may be correlated, making actual reliability worse.

**「Growth Impact」** The only quantified outcome is Klarna&\#x27;s 2024 AI customer-service assistant, which reportedly handled about two-thirds of customer conversations in its first month \(roughly 700 FTEs of work\) with a projected ~$40 million profit improvement—a projection the author flags as unverified. **「Takeaway」** Before greenlighting an AI initiative, write down the user&\#x27;s real business outcome and the three constraints your current process takes for granted, then ask which of those constraints new technology has actually removed—and fund a staged experiment only if it can cheaply change your next investment decision.
